# About

I am Marcin Biszczanik, a Cloud and Platform Consultant working in Azure,
infrastructure as code, and DevOps, based in Poland.

I help enterprises design Azure platforms and migrate to the cloud without
losing control of what they build. My focus is the hard-to-reverse part of
cloud work: landing zone design, identity, governance, and infrastructure as
code. My background is platform engineering and DevOps at enterprise scale:
hybrid estates of several hundred VMs and App Services in 24/7 environments,
identity governance for 10,000+ users across hybrid Active Directory and
Entra ID, and zero-downtime migrations of business-critical workloads. I am
certified as an Azure Solutions Architect Expert (AZ-305), Azure Network
Engineer Associate (AZ-700), and Azure Administrator Associate (AZ-104).

## What this blog is about

This blog documents the decisions behind Azure platform work, not just the
implementation. Most technical content explains how to deploy something; I am
more interested in why one option wins over another, what the trade-offs were,
and what I would decide differently next time. Expect articles on Azure
Verified Modules, landing zones, Bicep, IaC governance, migrations, and the
methods I use to make and record those decisions.

The reasoning I publish here is backed by working artifacts, all public:

- [azure-architecture-decisions](https://github.com/mbiszczanik/azure-architecture-decisions):
  my architecture decision records, written as reusable decision frameworks.
- [azure-bicep-modules](https://github.com/mbiszczanik/azure-bicep-modules):
  an opinionated, AVM-first Bicep module library, including the
  secure-hub-spoke-avnm pattern module.
- [skycraft](https://github.com/mbiszczanik/skycraft): a hands-on Azure
  learning project with an architecture commentary layer explaining the
  lab-versus-production gaps.

I also build and teach in public: I develop open Azure course material and
co-organize the Microsoft Azure User Group in Rzeszów, Poland.

## A note on anonymization

I work with enterprise clients under confidentiality obligations. Nothing on
this blog describes a specific client, engagement, or environment. Examples
come from my own public repositories or are written as generic patterns. Where
I cite facts about the Azure ecosystem, I date them, because the ecosystem
moves faster than blog posts age.
